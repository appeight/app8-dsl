Patch release on 0.7.18. All SDKs move to 0.7.19 together (iOS, Android, web renderer). `DSL_VERSION` stays 1.1. No new keys. Text with a fixed line height and decorated text render slightly differently, to match the web renderer.

- Fixed line height ([styles.md](../docs/styles.md#lineheight-types)): a `fixed` line height splits its leading evenly above and below each line's glyphs, as CSS half-leading does, on every engine. Text with a fixed line height above the font's natural height moves a few points down on iOS and Android (14 pt text on a 22 pt line: about 2.7 pt) and now sits where the web renderer draws it; a line height below the natural height no longer cuts the glyphs off, and on Android a tight one-line label is as tall as its line height, so content below it moves up. `multiplier` is unchanged.
- Underline and strikethrough ([styles.md](../docs/styles.md#underline)): on every line the decoration starts at the first glyph and ends at the last one, also on wrapped lines, before a line break, and on a truncated line, where the last glyph is the ellipsis. Letter spacing after a line's last glyph is no longer part of the line or of the label's width, so a decorated label with letter spacing is narrower by that spacing and a separate rule view sized to the label matches the text.
- Android draws underline and strikethrough itself, so they end at the glyphs on every Android version (14 and lower drew them past the last glyph by half the letter spacing, and past the ellipsis on truncated lines). Pickers and text fields keep the platform's own decoration.
- iOS draws its own ellipsis on truncated text that has an underline or strikethrough, so the decoration ends under the ellipsis.
- Vertical pager ([collections.md](../docs/collections.md#vertical-paging-a-full-screen-feed)): with `pagingStyle: "paging"` and `itemHeight: "fill"`, pages follow a change of the collection's frame height: they are measured again and the pager rests on a page boundary, also when the frame settles one layout pass late or changes during a drag or a deceleration. Before, on iOS, the pages could rest offset by the change.
